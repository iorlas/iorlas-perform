## 1. Scene economy

Sourced
- Scripted stage sketch = 3 parts: ~1 min exposition + setup, then conflict between heroes (usually from mutual misunderstanding), then climax. [БРЭ via search](https://bigenc.ru/c/sketch-shou-74b5ee) (Russian, stage sketches generally, not KVN)
- Sketch craft: "structure, escalation, and knowing exactly when to get out". [search summary of sketch guides](https://presentationbuilder.internationaltrucks.com/write-sketch) (low trust)
- Harmon's Story Circle was created so filmmakers can tell a story in ~5 min (Channel 101 pilots under 5 min); 8 steps: You, Need, Go, Search/Struggle, Find, Take/Suffer, Return, Change. [Boords](https://boords.com/blog/storytelling-101-the-dan-harmon-story-circle), [AutoCrit](https://www.autocrit.com/dan-harmon-story-circle-steps/). Origin claim is from secondary blogs, not Harmon's post.
- Sitcom threads: half-hour = A story + B story + a runner; the C runner is often only 2-3 scenes. Two-act older sitcoms fit only A+B. [TV Calling](https://www.tv-calling.com/what-are-a-b-and-c-stories-in-screenwriting-tv-writing-101/), [Creative Screenwriting](https://www.creativescreenwriting.com/cswcms/creating-a-b-and-c-storylines-for-tv-series/)
- Seinfeld kept callbacks inside one episode, bringing it "full circle" or giving an ironic twist ending. [Wikipedia Callback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Callback_(comedy)) (article flagged "needs citations")

Rules derived
- R1.1 One premise, one "game", one main location per 3-6 min piece. Story Circle fits 5 min, so map the episode to its 8 steps first; each scene must carry >=1 step. [Boords](https://boords.com/blog/storytelling-101-the-dan-harmon-story-circle) + [assumption] on mapping to scenes.
- R1.2 Budget [assumption]: 3-min piece = 2-3 scenes; 5-6 min = 3-4 scenes, max. Ep.03 has 6 in ~4.5 min: ~45 s/scene, which is the length of one exposition (БРЭ: first part alone ~1 min).
- R1.3 A scene change earns its place only if it does one of [assumption, synthesis of Story Circle "Go"/"Return" + BBC/ACX advice in craft.md]:
  - crosses a Story Circle threshold (Go, Return, Change)
  - time jump that makes the payoff possible ("a week later")
  - a new character whose arrival is the joke
  - a reveal the old location cannot hold
  Otherwise merge: move the beat into an existing location and let a character walk in/phone in.
- R1.4 Audio: each new place costs listener orientation (craft.md s.3). Every cut therefore "spends" ~10-15 s of non-joke material [assumption]. Count cuts x 12 s; if > 15% of runtime, merge scenes.
- R1.5 Phone/voice-in is a free location (no cut). Ep.03 already uses phones; use them for more beats instead of new rooms. [assumption]
- R1.6 A B-plot/runner gets 2-3 appearances total, not its own scene each time: attach it to an A-scene. [TV Calling](https://www.tv-calling.com/what-are-a-b-and-c-stories-in-screenwriting-tv-writing-101/)

Diagnosis of ep.03 [assumption, from reading the file]
- Scenes: Завод/утро, Серверная/ночь, Цех/утро, Проходная/вечер, Переговорка, Через неделю. Scene 4 (Михалыч, empathy) and scene 3 (Стас) are B-material hanging as separate rooms; neither feeds the final payoff, so both are candidates to merge or cut.
- Candidate: 3 scenes = (1) setup: Палыч orders, Вадик wishes at night (merge via phone call into the server room), (2) the factory with ads, Стас and Михалыч walk in, (3) boardroom payoff + Мама.

## 2. Joke architecture

### 2.1 Game of the scene (UCB)
- Game = "the first unusual thing to happen in the scene and how it will go on to define the scene"; alt. definition: a pattern from the relationship between characters. Cited to Besser, UCB Comedy Improvisation Manual pp.61-68, 81; heightening pp.112-154. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_of_the_scene) (summary, not manual text)
- Steps: ground who/what/where and play straight; find the first unusual thing; agree and commit; keep one unusual element ("crazy on top of crazy" confuses); ask "if this is true, what else is true?"; each return heightened (faster pace, higher stakes); return ~3 times "often but not always". [Script Magazine / Brett Wean](https://scriptmag.com/features/improvising-screenplays-finding-funny-screenplay-using-game-scene)
- Characters play "at the top of their intelligence", realistically; don't chase jokes, humour comes from reactions. [same](https://scriptmag.com/features/improvising-screenplays-finding-funny-screenplay-using-game-scene)
- Rules for us:
  - R2.1 Name the game of each scene in one sentence: "X does Y, and each time Z gets worse". If you cannot, the scene is a transition, not a comic scene. [assumption on the test, concept sourced]
  - R2.2 One game per episode (two max: A and B). Ep.03 has at least five unrelated gags (QR/тред, tariff/ads, Стас крыса-кун, Михалыч, пустые банки/Мама). [assumption]
  - R2.3 The game's "first unusual thing" should arrive in <= ~45 s [assumption].

### 2.2 Rule of three, escalation
- Rule of three: two beats set a pattern, the third "cuts across the line, it defeats the expectation". [Vorhaus via No Film School](https://nofilmschool.com/john-vorhaus-comedy-writing-tools)
- Game returns three times, each heightened. [Script Magazine](https://scriptmag.com/features/improvising-screenplays-finding-funny-screenplay-using-game-scene)
- Runners in sketch shows typically come in three parts; commenters call it a pattern not a law, and warn against 3 sketches of the same joke varied trivially. [BCG forum](https://www.comedy.co.uk/forums/post/1057565/) (forum, low trust)
- R2.4 Each repeat must change one thing (stakes, speaker, medium, scale). Beat 1 plants, beat 2 confirms the pattern, beat 3 breaks it or scales it. [synthesis, sourced pieces above]

### 2.3 Callbacks and runners
- Callback = joke referring back to material "previously told in the set"; depends on the audience having met the original. Running gag = repeated callbacks to the same joke. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Callback_(comedy))
- Runner: a funny base joke, repeated with variations as it grows familiar; bait-and-switch allowed; humour also from fit/clash with the context; a trivial line does not become a runner by repetition. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Running_gag)
- Practitioner guideline: a joke comes back at most twice (3 total); the third is a bookend. [search summary of comedy blogs](https://zeroes.pages.dev/c/comedy/anatomy-of-a-joke) (low trust)
- Seinfeld pattern: callbacks live inside one episode (self-contained, safe for out-of-order viewing). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Callback_(comedy))
- Callback rules (checkable):
  - R2.5 Setup must be heard IN THIS episode. Series-level callbacks are bonus for fans, never the only payoff. [sourced: Seinfeld in-episode practice; "audience must have met original"] This is the ep.03 failure: «нет» is only in the series intro.
  - R2.6 Distance: setup within the last ~60-90 s before payoff, or re-touched in between (a "reminder"). [assumption; no source gives numbers; listener cannot scroll back]
  - R2.7 Recognisable: payoff reuses the setup's exact key words (same phrase, same voice, same sound). [assumption, consistent with "audience has met the original"]
  - R2.8 Setup is planted as a throwaway (inside another purpose: an insult, an order), not flagged. Payoff re-frames its meaning. [assumption]
  - R2.9 Rule-of-three for the callback: plant, echo (variant), payoff-with-twist. Don't use the intro joke more than twice more than the plant. [assumption + BCG forum]
- Concrete fix for ep.03 [assumption]: inside the episode, make "answered «нет»" a visible event: e.g. in scene 1 Палыч asks Вадика "Подпишешь?" and Вадик says «нет», pause, narrator or Стас mouths the rhyme; echo once in the factory (Стас or Михалыч about it); then the AI ad in the boardroom lands because all 5 suits and the listener know. Spell out the rhyme only once (narrator "рифму знали все"), as ep.03 already does.

### 2.4 Button endings
- Sourced advice on endings is thin. Seinfeld uses a full-circle callback or ironic twist as the ending. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Callback_(comedy)); a set opening with a premise and closing on it "feels complete". [search summary of comedy-writing site](https://comedipedia.pages.dev/blog/joke-writing-tips) (low trust)
- R2.10 Final line = callback to the episode's first unusual thing, or the A-plot's cost made personal (ep.03: Мама "Ну я и подписалась" already works because «Подпишись» was planted a line earlier). [assumption + sourced bookend]
- R2.11 Nothing after the button except sting/music. [assumption]

## 3. Pacing and flow

Sourced
- Joke density is a style choice, not a quality metric. Informal counts: Father Knows Best ~2.5 jokes/min, M*A*S*H ~4, The Office >5 (6.65 in another count), Friends 6.06, Big Bang 5.80, Kimmy ~7. [Cracked via search](https://www.cracked.com/article_31798_has-comedy-reached-its-speed-limit.html), [Medium](https://medium.com/@vikram.venkat/the-one-with-too-many-jokes-9cb32288f463). Fan counts, definitions vary.
- Multi-cam spec guideline: at least 3 jokes per page. [search summary](https://www.perlego.com/es/book/1573773/tv-writing-on-demand-creating-great-content-in-the-digital-era-pdf) (low trust)
- Ken Levine: fewer jokes can be funnier; on Cheers/Frasier he would go a full page without a joke to set up a payoff; "three genuine big laughs" beat twenty zingers; laughs also come from attitudes, pauses, reactions; machine-gun joke rate feels "forced, exhausting". [Levine blog](https://kenlevine.blogspot.com/2017/07/laughs-per-minute.html)
- Comic relief = releasing tension; comedy needs tension to release. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comic_relief) (thin)

Rules
- R3.1 Target [assumption, from the 2.5-7 jpm range and TTS delivery slower than live]: 3-4 jokes/min for a narrated piece => 10-20 jokes in 4-5 min, of which 3-5 are "big" (Levine: big laughs matter most). Ep.03 reads as ~15 small gags and few big ones.
- R3.2 Plan 3-5 BIG beats (one per Story Circle turn: Go, Find, Suffer, Return/Change) and let smaller jokes serve them. [assumption mapping]
- R3.3 After a big laugh: 1-2 s silence or music sting before the next line, and the next line is NOT a joke (a reaction or plain information). [assumption; craft.md has pause values, Levine supports "pauses and reactions"]
- R3.4 Setup stretches are legal: allow ~20-30 s without a joke if it plants a payoff that becomes a big beat. [Levine's "full page without a joke"; seconds are assumption]
- R3.5 Chain rule: each joke should cause or enable the next (the reaction to joke N is joke N+1; the cost of joke N becomes the setup of N+1). Test: delete any joke; if the next one still makes sense unchanged, the chain is not causal. [assumption; "if this is true, what else is true?" is the sourced mechanism: [Script Magazine](https://scriptmag.com/features/improvising-screenplays-finding-funny-screenplay-using-game-scene)]
- R3.6 Three kinds of connection to use, strongest first [assumption]:
  1. Consequence: previous line's outcome triggers the next.
  2. Same game, next heightening.
  3. Callback to an earlier planted line.
  Weakest and what ep.03 mostly does: new unrelated gag in a new place (topic change).
- R3.7 A/B in a short: B-plot shares the A-plot's theme from another angle and is usually the same subject; C-runner mirrors A even if lightly. [TV Calling](https://www.tv-calling.com/what-are-a-b-and-c-stories-in-screenwriting-tv-writing-101/), [Creative Screenwriting](https://www.creativescreenwriting.com/cswcms/creating-a-b-and-c-storylines-for-tv-series/). In 4-5 min: A (Вадик vs the ad machine) + one runner (Мама subscription: appear twice, resolve in button). Drop further threads. [assumption]
- R3.8 Runner (C) enters after the A-story's first turn (B-stories enter later, often after the first act break). [TV Calling](https://www.tv-calling.com/what-are-a-b-and-c-stories-in-screenwriting-tv-writing-101/)
- R3.9 Escalation curve: each big beat bigger than previous. Last beat = highest stakes + personal cost (Story Circle "Suffer"/"Change"). [Boords](https://boords.com/blog/storytelling-101-the-dan-harmon-story-circle)

## 4. Character and world design for recurring comedy

### 4.1 Character bible fields
- A circulating Vorhaus-attributed list: flaw; why the audience forgives the flaw; comic filter/perspective (how they see the world); want; need; exaggeration. Attribution disputed in the thread. [BCG forum](https://www.comedy.co.uk/forums/post/998902/) (low trust), [Vorhaus interview excerpt on comic filters](https://id.player.fm/1BeAJZf)
- "A comic character has a way of looking at the world"; the filter drives action. Opposite filters (obey rules / break rules) produce clashes. [No Film School](https://nofilmschool.com/john-vorhaus-comedy-writing-tools)
- Comic opposites: give A a rule, give B the opposite rule, lock them in a place and "make them fight about stuff". [same](https://nofilmschool.com/john-vorhaus-comedy-writing-tools)
- Inappropriate response: every situation has few appropriate behaviours and infinitely many inappropriate ones; characters are funny when their filter forces the inappropriate one. [same](https://nofilmschool.com/john-vorhaus-comedy-writing-tools)
- Catchphrases are related to but not the same as running gags; a runner needs a funny base. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Running_gag)

Template for our cast (fields) [assumption: synthesis of above]
```
id / voice:
want (this episode, concrete object):
need (what they lack):
flaw + why we forgive it:
comic filter (one sentence: "sees everything as ..."):
inappropriate response (their default wrong move):
what they do TO others (function: pushes / blocks / exposes / flatters):
status default (high/low) and how it flips:
verbal signature: ONE rhythm or tic (catchphrase max 1, used <= 1x per ep unless it is the runner):
sound signature (craft.md s.1-2):
```

### 4.2 Relationship matrix
- Status: relative power between characters, shifting moment to moment; a seesaw; "get the status right and the scene plays itself"; not social class and not jerk-vs-victim. [Johnstone, Impro, via summaries](https://scriptreader.ai/guides/impro-johnstone), [Buteau](https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-from-keith-johnstone/) (secondary)
- Matrix rule [assumption, synthesis]: for each pair that shares a scene, write one row:
```
A -> B: wants ___ from B; plays status HIGH/LOW; the flip moment = ___
B -> A: wants ___ from A; ...
conflict engine = A's filter vs B's filter (comic opposites)
```
- R4.1 Every scene needs one pair with opposite filters or opposite wants; others are witnesses. [Vorhaus above + assumption]
- R4.2 Status must change at least once per scene (the seesaw flips on the joke). [Johnstone via summaries]
- R4.3 Cast size per episode: 3 voiced characters needed to carry the A+runner; each extra voice must own a joke nobody else can do, else cut. [assumption; craft.md already caps at 4/scene] Ep.03 uses 5 + narrator + AI: Стас and Михалыч each get one scene of non-connected material.

### 4.3 World entities (objects/places/institutions) by function
- No sourced framework found. [assumption]: for each entity record its plot function and kill it if it has none:
  - Plant-and-payoff object: a thing introduced early that returns later (the banka, the ad speaker, «Зинаида»).
  - Pressure source: institution that issues deadlines/orders (совет директоров, Палыч).
  - Rule-machine: entity with a fixed absurd rule that generates the game (AI "free tier with ads"; literal wishes). One rule, stated once, obeyed always. Comic worlds run on a single consistently applied rule. [Script Magazine: one unusual thing, then keep it](https://scriptmag.com/features/improvising-screenplays-finding-funny-screenplay-using-game-scene)
  - Place = mood + sound; reuse places across episodes (recurring set), invent new ones only when the joke needs the space.
- R4.4 Recurring-series reuse: series-level jokes (intro rhyme) are assets, but each episode re-plants what it needs [see R2.5].

## 5. Pre-render checklist for a script doctor

Run in order; any "no" = fix before render.

Structure
1. Can the premise + game be stated in two sentences ("X wants Y; the unusual thing Z; it gets worse by ...")?
2. Scene count <= 3 (<=4 for 5-6 min)? Each scene crosses a Story Circle step or is the only way to deliver a reveal?
3. Could any scene be a phone call or a character walking in instead?
4. A-plot + at most one runner; the runner mirrors the A theme?
5. Does each scene end with a turn (decision, reveal, cost) and is its game heightened vs previous scene?

Jokes
6. List every callback. For each: plant exists in THIS episode? plant is within ~90 s or re-touched? payoff reuses exact words? payoff adds a twist? (Fail any = rewrite.)
7. Series-intro gags: is there also a stand-alone in-episode plant?
8. Each repeating gag: appears 3 times max, changes each time, third one breaks or scales?
9. Chain test: delete any joke in the middle; does the next line break? (Should.) Count jokes that are topic changes: <= 2.
10. 3-5 big beats marked; escalating; the biggest is last or second last.
11. Fewer than ~4 jokes/min on average; at least one 20 s plant stretch with no joke.

Cast
12. Every speaking character has a want in this episode and a distinct comic filter? Anyone with no function = cut.
13. Each scene has one pair in conflict (opposite want/filter) and a status flip.
14. Catchphrases <= 1 per character per episode.
15. Entities (objects, institutions) each have a plot function; each stated rule is used again.

Ending
16. Last line is a bookend to the first unusual thing, in <= 8 words, with room for a sting (craft.md checklist 14).
17. Read aloud eyes-closed: at each cut, do you know where and when you are? (craft.md checklist 15)

## 6. Episode structures for shorts

Gathered 2026-10-28 by web search + Wikipedia fetch. Harmon circle: see s.0/1, not repeated. Search-summary sources are secondary; runtime shares are [assumption] unless stated. No source found giving beat percentages for a 3-6 min piece.

### 6.1 Candidate structures

**Sitcom (22 min half-hour)**
- Order: cold open/teaser, acts, tag. Teaser = 1-5 min mini-act before the credits. [TV Tropes Teaser via search](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/Teaser)
- 22-24 min runtime, acts originally cut by ad breaks; 60s-80s = teaser + 2 acts + tag; today often cold open + 4 acts. [NoFilmSchool](https://nofilmschool.com/how-to-write-a-sitcom), [Fiveable summary](https://fiveable.me/tv-writing/unit-1/sitcoms/study-guide/6Idkg9KvIDaOywMa) (study guides, low trust)
- Tag: short scene after main story; sometimes a cliffhanger/next-episode setup. [same Fiveable]
- Escalation: a "point of no return" ~3/4 through, where nothing can get worse, forces the exit; "make your characters make things worse". [BCG sitcom rules](https://comedy.co.uk/pro/inside_track/the-10-rules-of-sitcom-writing), [BCG scenes](https://comedy.co.uk/pro/inside_track/how-to-structure-sitcom-scenes)
- Schur: same beat twice in a row bores; beat 2 must be bigger. [Ferriss page](https://rosetta.to/u/timferriss/the-fundamentals-of-sitcom-writing-and-teaching-moments-from-the-office)
- Shrunk to 4.5 min [assumption]: 1 mini-act of cold open + 1 act + tag. Act breaks drop out (they were ad breaks, not craft); keep the 3/4 no-return point.

**Freytag's pyramid (exposition, rising action, climax/peripeteia, falling action, denouement)**
- 5 stages from tragedy, Freytag 1863; peripeteia sits AT the climax; "catastrophe" was Freytag's word for the end, "denouement" is later. [Dramatic structure mirror](https://bafybeiaysi4s6lnjev27ln5icwm6tueaw2vdykrtjkwiphwekaywqhcjze.ipfs.ipfs.hypha.coop/wiki/Dramatic_structure), [Utrecht dramaturgy DB](https://dramaturgydatabase.hum.uu.nl/record/die-technik-des-dramas/)
- Freytag did not treat comedy; modern writers apply it to comedy anyway. [same mirror]
- Why falling action shrinks in comedy [assumption, synthesis]: after the biggest laugh nothing is left to pay; sources say sketches end on a button/blackout right after the peak (6.1 sketch), Seinfeld ends on callback or ironic twist (s.2.4). So: falling action = 1 line (reaction) + button; denouement = the button itself.

**Kishōtenketsu (4 beats, 4-panel yonkoma)**
- Ki intro (characters, setting); Shō development (follows leads toward the twist, no major change); Ten twist = the crux/climax; Ketsu conclusion (also "ochi", the ending). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kish%C5%8Dtenketsu)
- Not conflict-centred; conflict can be present (Parasite). Withholds a major element until Ten, which can shift the genre. [Wikipedia]
- Twist = reframing, not a complication. [search summary](https://filmlifestyle.com/what-is-kishotenketsu/) (blog, low trust)
- Fit [assumption]: perfect for a gag where the reveal recontextualises the first two beats (e.g. what looked like X was Y). Weak when the engine is two characters fighting.

**Sketch (premise, heightening, peak, button)**
- "What if?" premise = the game; first unusual thing, repeats bigger, causes a real problem, peaks. Heightening = consequences worsen, not volume. [search summary of sketch guides](https://www.skillshare.com/en/blog/how-to-write-sketch-comedy-a-guide/) (low trust)
- Ending names: blowout (peak absurdity) then blackout (final joke/exit); button = quick funny ending; reversal/twist when audience thinks it knows the pattern. [same summary]
- Stage sketch: ~1 min exposition + setup, then conflict, then climax. [БРЭ](https://bigenc.ru/c/sketch-shou-74b5ee) (s.1)
- Game, heightening, rule of three: s.2.1-2.2, not repeated.

**Pixar / Story Spine (8 sentences)**
- Once upon a time ___. Every day ___. But one day ___. Because of that ___. (repeat) Because of that ___. Until finally ___. And ever since then ___. Optional: "the moral is ___". [SessionLab](https://www.sessionlab.com/methods/story-spine), [NPR interview with inventor](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/nx-s1-5750619)
- Origin: Kenn Adams (improv, Synergy Theater); reached Pixar via Rebecca Stockley teaching improv there. Adams says Pixar's success does not depend on it. [NPR](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/nx-s1-5750619) (one blog credits Emma Coats; unconfirmed)
- Fit: strong as a 1-line-per-beat pitch test. "Because of that" chain = causal escalation = our R3.5 chain rule. [assumption on link]

**Save the Cat compressed**
- Screenplay targets (110 p): opening image p1, theme stated p5, set-up p1-10, catalyst p12, debate p12-25, break into two p25; novel version: set-up 1-10%, catalyst ~10%, midpoint 50%, finale 80-99%, final image 99-100%. Treat as guardrails. [Filmdaft](https://filmdaft.com/save-the-cat-beat-sheet/), [Reedsy](https://blog.reedsy.com/guide/story-structure/save-the-cat-beat-sheet)
- All is Lost ~p75 (~68%) is from general knowledge, not confirmed by the fetched sources.
- Compression [assumption]: catalyst at ~10% = 27 s of 270 s; midpoint 50% = 2:15; all-is-lost ~68% = 3:03; finale from 80% = 3:36. In 4.5 min drop Debate, Fun and Games, B-story, Dark Night as separate beats; keep opening image, catalyst, midpoint, all-is-lost, finale, final image.

**Anime/shonen episode**
- Segment order (fan diagram): recap, cold open (avant-title), OP (~1.5 min), Part A, eyecatch/ad break, Part B, ED, optional after-credits scene, next-episode preview (PV). [japanesewithanime diagram](https://media.japanesewithanime.com/images/1738/parts-of-an-anime-episode.png/), [Anime Glossary](https://wotaku.netlify.app/glossary/anime)
- No source gave the minutes split of a 24-min episode. [gap]
- Take-aways [assumption]: Part A ends on a turn that makes you stay through the break (mid-episode hook); next-episode preview = a promise, not a plot; episodes are standalone-ish arcs inside a larger one. Fixed OP/eyecatch/ED are branding, not craft; audio equivalent is a signature sting.

### 6.2 What suits 3-6 min audio (verdicts)
- Kishōtenketsu: DO for single-gag episodes (reveal in beat 3 reframes beats 1-2). REFUSE as the only engine for dialogue comedy with 3-5 voices; no conflict means no scene-to-scene fuel.
- Sketch (premise, 3x heightening, peak, button): DO, backbone. Matches game-of-scene (s.2.1), already adopted in this file.
- Story Spine: DO as a 8-line pitch/check before writing, not as runtime allocation.
- Freytag: pitch-level only. Five stages with falling action cannot fit in 4.5 min; keep exposition + rising + peak, fold the rest into the button.
- Save the Cat: borrow midpoint (reversal at ~50%) and all-is-lost; do not use the 15 beats.
- Sitcom: borrow cold open (hook) + tag + the 3/4 point of no return; drop acts.
- Anime: borrow Part A/B hook at midpoint and the signature sting; skip the rest.

Recommended hybrid: sketch spine + kishōtenketsu twist + sitcom hook/tag. Shares for 270 s [assumption; all numbers untested, calibrate by ear]:
```
0:00  15s  6%   HOOK/teaser   narrator: the unusual thing or the want, in 1-2 lines (cold open)
0:15  30s 11%   SETUP (ki)    who, want, the one rule of the world; plant the Chekhov object/phrase
0:45  15s  5%   INCITING      "but one day": the unusual thing hits (game starts)
1:00 125s 46%   LADDER (shō)  3 heightenings, ~40s each: plan -> complication -> worse; each caused by the last
                              (beat 2 around 2:15 = midpoint reversal; point of no return ~3:20)
3:05  45s 17%   TURN (ten)    peak + twist/peripeteia; payoff of the planted object
3:50  20s  7%   BUTTON (ketsu) reaction line + final line, <= 8 words
4:10  20s  7%   TAG/STINGER  runner or next-episode hint, then sting
4:30
```
- Falling action = 1 reaction line inside the button, not a scene.
- Scale for 3 min: ladder 2 beats; 6 min: 4 beats, never a second game.

### 6.3 Series-level devices
- Cliffhanger vs closed: cliffhanger = unresolved precarious situation at end of an instalment to bring audiences back; film serials resolved it at the start of the next; American primetime rarely used them before 1980 (Dallas "Who shot J.R."); some shows died unresolved (Twin Peaks). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cliffhanger)
- Reset button: sitcoms restore status quo so episodes can be rerun in any order (syndication); less common once streaming/story arcs spread. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reset_button_technique), [TV Tropes](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/ResetButton)
- Verdict for us [assumption]: closed episode + reset by default (listener may start anywhere; payoff must be in-episode, s.2.5). Serial hook lives ONLY in the tag, as a question, never as a needed plot resolution. A cliffhanger inside a 4.5 min piece costs the listener a week of waiting for a joke; REFUSE as default.
- Runner/end tag across episodes: s.2.3, s.3.7, s.3.8. Tag = place for the cross-episode runner. [assumption]
- Chekhov's gun: an element should be necessary; never show the loaded rifle unless it fires; modern use = early element whose significance emerges later. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chekhov%27s_gun) Gun pays off; foreshadowing may not; an unpaid setup reads as a red herring; keep the plant low-key. [Jericho Writers](https://jerichowriters.com/what-is-chekhovs-gun/), [Write Practice](https://thewritepractice.com/checkovs-gun/)
- MacGuffin: object/event that drives plot and motivates characters, little significance itself; Hitchcock: spies want it, audience does not care. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacGuffin) Use [assumption]: the thing everyone chases in the ladder; the joke is what chasing does, so keep its identity trivial and interchangeable across episodes.
- Ticking clock/deadline: BCG point of no return at ~3/4 gives a built-in deadline shape. [BCG](https://comedy.co.uk/pro/inside_track/the-10-rules-of-sitcom-writing) Audio use [assumption]: state the deadline in the setup in a sound-able way (time announcements, a countdown voice).
- Reversal (peripeteia) and reveal (anagnorisis): Freytag places peripeteia at the climax. [mirror](https://bafybeiaysi4s6lnjev27ln5icwm6tueaw2vdykrtjkwiphwekaywqhcjze.ipfs.ipfs.hypha.coop/wiki/Dramatic_structure) Anagnorisis: no fetched source; [assumption] = a character or the listener learns the true identity/meaning (a ten-beat reveal in kishōtenketsu terms).
- Rule of three (comic triple): two items set pattern, third breaks it, third funniest, quick timing. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rule_of_three_(writing))
- Fair-play twist: real clues given, solution obvious in hindsight; ideal reveal "surprising and inevitable"; reversals in comedy must be set up and paid off almost instantly to get a laugh. [17 Rules of Mystery](https://medium.com/the-coffeelicious/17-rules-of-mystery-89cc35cda659), [mystery conventions](https://pvliesdonk.github.io/if-craft-corpus/corpus-docs/genre-conventions/mystery_conventions), [Cheeky Monkey comic storytelling](https://www.cheekymonkeycomedy.com/?p=299)
- Twist test [assumption]: the clue is spoken in setup as a throwaway, in the first 60 s of the episode, within ~90 s of the payoff or re-touched; a listener replaying the episode should hear it.

### 6.4 Expanding one idea into an episode
- Sourced: escalation follows the story's own logic and the characters' established behaviour; make characters make things worse; predictability is the enemy of escalation. [BCG rules](https://comedy.co.uk/pro/inside_track/the-10-rules-of-sitcom-writing), [Comedy Bizarre](https://comedybizarre.substack.com/p/how-to-make-your-humor-heighten)
- Improv "if this is true, what else is true?" and agree-and-commit: s.2.1 (Script Magazine). "Yes, and" itself: no source fetched here; [assumption] it is that agree-and-commit step.
- Series expansion (One Piece etc.): no source found for how a long-running series expands an idea. [gap]
- Escalation ladder worksheet [assumption, synthesis of the above]:
```
premise      : X wants Y, but the world has rule R
rung 1 (plan): X tries the obvious way; R makes it cost something small
rung 2       : X fixes it with a worse move (character's inappropriate response); cost grows, a second person is dragged in
rung 3       : fix #2 backfires at scale; point of no return
turn         : the planted object / phrase pays off; the situation is reframed (ten)
button       : cost made personal, <= 8 words
```
- Each rung must be caused by the previous one (chain rule R3.5), change one dimension only (stakes, people, scale, medium; R2.4), and the worst thing that could happen to THIS character, not generic disaster. Test: pitch it as a Story Spine; if a "because of that" is really "and then", rewrite.
- Generating the premise [assumption]: pick the character's filter (s.4.1), pick the rule R that most punishes it, list 10 consequences of R, order by size; the best 3 are rungs, the 11th (the one that reframes) is the twist.
