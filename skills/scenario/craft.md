# Audio drama craft: who, where, when

> Sources for radio-drama craft. The rules themselves live in `SKILL.md`; where a number here differs
> from SKILL.md, SKILL.md wins: it is tuned by ear on rendered episodes.

Sourced rules behind the scenario skill and the lint in `scripts/scenario.py`, gathered 2026-10-28.

Coverage note: bbc.co.uk was blocked to my fetcher; Crook, Hand, Teddern (book), Night Vale and The Truth text were not retrievable. Rules below come from reachable sources. "[assumption]" marks numbers no source gives; test by ear.

## 1. First appearance of a speaker
- Name a new voice in narration just before the first line; audio has no "he said". [Thorpe](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/14739435-top-tips-for-creating-an-audio-drama)
- Introduce characters one at a time (Dragnet model); cap at 4 speakers per scene. [Stage 32](https://stage32.com/blog/Four-Crucial-Guidelines-for-Writing-Compelling-Audio-Drama)
- Plan 3-4 major speaking roles, 6 at most. [Thorpe](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/14739435-top-tips-for-creating-an-audio-drama)
- Large cast: names matter most when the next speaker is unclear; small, contrasting cast: voices carry it. [ADWIT](https://www.buzzsprout.com/1726329/episodes/17000530-character-breakdowns-that-jump-off-the-page)
- Add a sound signature (footsteps, clank, mic proximity, reverb). [Writer's Digest](https://writersdigest.com/world-building-with-sound-10-audio-tips-for-fiction-writers), [ADWIT](https://www.buzzsprout.com/1726329/episodes/17000530-character-breakdowns-that-jump-off-the-page)
- Limit characters in a scene, or how many speak. [ACX/Lindstrom](https://www.acx.com/mp/blog/acx-guest-post-wendy-lindstrom-on-writing-for-audio)
- No source gives a deadline in seconds. [assumption] Name within the first 1-2 lines after entry.

## 2. Re-identifying later
- Give each character a tic, repeated phrase or sentence rhythm no one else shares. [ADWIT](https://www.buzzsprout.com/1726329/episodes/17000530-character-breakdowns-that-jump-off-the-page)
- Repeat the same sound cue on every entrance. [Writer's Digest](https://writersdigest.com/world-building-with-sound-10-audio-tips-for-fiction-writers)
- Same-gender voices in rapid exchange need tags. [ACX/Lindstrom](https://www.acx.com/mp/blog/acx-guest-post-wendy-lindstrom-on-writing-for-audio)
- [assumption] After a scene change or long absence, treat the character as new: re-name in the first line.

## 3. Scene transitions
- Signal every change of place/time. "If you confuse, you lose." [Ruya Sonic](https://ruyasonic.com/wrt_time_transitions.htm)
- Slug lines (scene, place, time) stay unspoken, for cast and crew only. [Ruya Sonic](https://ruyasonic.com/wrt_time_transitions.htm)
- Don't open a new scene with the character who closed the last one. [Ruya Sonic](https://ruyasonic.com/wrt_time_transitions.htm)
- Use a music curtain, a tone change, a narrator line, or a cross-fade. [Ruya Sonic](https://ruyasonic.com/wrt_time_transitions.htm)
- Ambience works as the "setting paragraph". [Writer's Digest](https://writersdigest.com/world-building-with-sound-10-audio-tips-for-fiction-writers)
- Set place in the first line of dialogue plus one sound. [BCG forum/Arrowsmith summary](https://www.firstwriter.com/articles/?GUID=3135)
- Start mid-action, leave once the purpose is served. [Thorpe](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/14739435-top-tips-for-creating-an-audio-drama) (Thorpe also says "prefer several short scenes"; superseded by the scene budget in SKILL.md, measured on a 6-scene episode that felt choppy.)
- Narration: if over word budget, "cut narration first". [Thorpe](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/14739435-top-tips-for-creating-an-audio-drama)

## 4. Pacing and silence
- Silence is a choice; no unintentional silences. Long pause before a confession; sudden stillness after loud. [Writer's Digest](https://writersdigest.com/world-building-with-sound-10-audio-tips-for-fiction-writers)
- A half-second gap can improve a joke. [Writer's Digest](https://writersdigest.com/world-building-with-sound-10-audio-tips-for-fiction-writers)
- Paragraph pause: too short is rushed, too long reads as scene change. Scene break: clearly longer, with fresh delivery after. No seconds given. [NarrationBox](https://narrationbox.com/blog/scene-break-section-pause-conventions-ai-audiobooks)
- Only hard numbers: ACX file edges, 0.5-1 s room tone at head, 1-5 s at tail. Not for in-story pauses. [NarrationBox](https://narrationbox.com/blog/scene-break-section-pause-conventions-ai-audiobooks)
- [assumption, test by ear] Line-to-line 0.3-0.6 s; dramatic beat 1-2 s; scene break 1.5-3 s plus ambience change; over ~3 s of dead air reads as a dropout.

## 5. Not feeling cut off
- Silence/light ambience can be the transition (The Archers). [Ruya Sonic](https://ruyasonic.com/wrt_time_transitions.htm)
- Stingers and whooshes bridge scenes. [SFX Engine](https://sfxengine.com/blog/sound-effects-for-drama)
- Pair a scene pause with fresh energy on the next line. [NarrationBox](https://narrationbox.com/blog/scene-break-section-pause-conventions-ai-audiobooks)
- Gap: no reachable source on "last line that lands". [assumption] End on a decision, image or reveal, then a stinger or room tone.

## 6. Narrator vs dialogue
- Distinct voices make extra tags like "he continued" distracting; without contrast, tags matter. [Writers Helping Writers](https://writershelpingwriters.net/2016/06/taking-audiobook-plunge-read-first/)
- Tag in fast or long exchanges. [ACX/Lindstrom](https://www.acx.com/mp/blog/acx-guest-post-wendy-lindstrom-on-writing-for-audio)
- Long narration gets boring; balance narration, dialogue, thought. [ACX/Lindstrom](https://www.acx.com/mp/blog/acx-guest-post-wendy-lindstrom-on-writing-for-audio)
- Signpost thoughts ("I thought"). [Driscoll](https://audreydriscoll.com/2024/12/29/audiobook-advice/)

## 7. Comedy timing
- Keep it tight; every word earns its place. [BCG Pro, "Keep up the pace"](https://comedy.co.uk/pro/features/six-of-the-best-tips-for-writing-radio-comedy)
- Practise inflection and pauses before the punchline. [BCG summary](https://www.firstwriter.com/articles/?GUID=3135)
- Sound effects can sell a gag. [BCG summary](https://www.firstwriter.com/articles/?GUID=3135)
- [assumption] Setup 1-3 lines, beat 0.5 s, punchline short (under ~8 words), last word the funny one.

## 8. Signposting joke context
- Use the blind medium: a late reveal of location is a radio joke (Conchords audition in a toilet). [Arrowsmith](https://www.firstwriter.com/articles/?GUID=3135)
- Put context in an earlier line or a sound, never in the joke. [assumption, from Writer's Digest ambience-as-setting](https://writersdigest.com/world-building-with-sound-10-audio-tips-for-fiction-writers)

## Checklist
1. Is every new voice named in the line before or within its first line?
2. At most 4 speakers in the scene?
3. Does each character have a unique tic or rhythm?
4. Is a returning character re-named after a scene break or long absence?
5. Does the first line of each scene give place or time?
6. Is there a sound or ambience change at each scene break?
7. Does a different character open the new scene than closed the last?
8. Is every pause intentional, none over ~3 s?
9. Do tags appear in fast or same-gender exchanges?
10. Is narration cut to the minimum?
11. Are thoughts signposted?
12. Does each scene end on a decision, image or reveal?
13. Is joke context planted earlier, not explained?
14. Does the punchline land on the last word?
15. Read aloud: can a listener follow with eyes closed?
